# Substitution Reference

Starting points, not laws. Always adjust for the specific recipe. Quantities are per the amount of original ingredient replaced.

## Meat replacements

| Original role | Good options | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ground meat (sauces, tacos, chili, stuffing) | TVP, crumbled extra-firm tofu, lentils, finely chopped mushrooms (or a mix) | TVP: rehydrate ~1:1 by volume dry TVP to hot vegetable broth, 5-10 min, then season assertively; it's mild and needs umami (soy sauce, tomato paste, smoked paprika). Tofu: press, crumble, brown in a dry or lightly oiled pan until dry and golden before adding sauce. Mushrooms add umami and moisture; lentils add earthiness and soften more. A mushroom + TVP or tofu mix is closest to the original. |
| Chicken pieces (stir-fry, curry, nuggets) | Extra-firm tofu cubes, tempeh, chickpeas, seitan | Press tofu 15+ min; a light cornstarch coat then air fry gives a crisp exterior. Tempeh: steam or simmer 10 min first to remove bitterness. Seitan is not gluten-free. |
| Sausage / bacon | Tempeh, smoked tofu, mushrooms, smoked paprika + liquid smoke seasoning | Rely on seasoning: fennel, paprika, garlic, and a little soy sauce. |
| Shredded meat (pulled pork, carnitas) | Young jackfruit (canned in brine, rinsed), shredded king oyster mushrooms | Rinse jackfruit well and cook longer to absorb sauce; it has little protein, so consider adding beans on the side. |
| Meatballs / burgers | Lentil, black bean, or chickpea patties bound with oats or breadcrumbs; TVP-based | Bake or air fry instead of pan-frying. Chill the mix so it holds. |
| Fish | Marinated tofu, hearts of palm, chickpeas (for "tuna" salads), nori for the sea flavor | Set expectations. It won't taste identical. |

Protein boosts (when the recipe has no meat to replace): add white beans or lentils to soups and sauces, swap in Greek yogurt for sour cream or mayo in part, use high-protein pasta or add chickpeas, stir silken tofu into creamy sauces or smoothies.

## Flours and grains

Rule of thumb: start with a **partial** swap so structure survives. Whole-grain and alt flours absorb more liquid and have less gluten.

| Flour | Swap level (for all-purpose) | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Whole wheat | Up to 50% in most baking; up to 100% in pancakes, muffins, quick breads with extra liquid (1-2 tbsp per cup) | Denser, nuttier. White whole wheat is milder. Let batter rest 10-15 min to hydrate the bran. |
| Buckwheat | 25-30% in yeast or structural baking; up to 50% in pancakes, crepes, quick breads | Earthy, slightly bitter; gluten-free, so it needs a gluten-containing flour or binder (egg, psyllium) to hold together. Great in pancakes and crepes. |
| Oat flour | Up to 30-40%; up to 100% in some cookies and muffins with a binder | Soft, mild, slightly sweet. Absorbs a lot of moisture. Blend rolled oats for a quick version. |
| Almond flour | 20-25% blended in; more only in recipes designed for it | No gluten, high fat, browns fast. Reduce oven temperature ~25°F for full swaps. Not for yeast breads. |

Other grain swaps: brown rice, quinoa or farro in place of white rice or pasta sides; whole-wheat or legume pasta for regular pasta (cook by taste, usually a minute or two different); cauliflower rice for part of the rice.

Yeast breads and delicate cakes (angel food, chiffon) are the most fragile. Offer a small swap or leave alone rather than promise a full one.

## Cooking method

- **Deep-fried to air-fried**: coat lightly (1-2 tsp oil sprayed or tossed on), cook 375-400°F. Typically 60-75% of oven time, shake or flip halfway. Wet batters don't work in an air fryer; switch to a dry breading (panko + spices, pressed on) and mention it.
- **Pan-fried to baked**: 425°F on a preheated, lightly oiled sheet pan, flip halfway. Wire rack over the pan crisps the underside.
- **Sautéing**: cut oil to 1-2 tsp and add a splash of water or broth as the pan dries. Use nonstick or well-seasoned cookware.
- **Roasting**: toss with a measured amount of oil (about 1 tsp per cup of vegetables) instead of drizzling freely.
- If the user may not own an air fryer, always give the oven equivalent.

## Oil and fat

- Cut oil by roughly 30-50% in savory dishes when a nonstick pan or added broth can cover it; keep enough to carry flavor in dressings and sauces.
- In baking, replace up to half the oil or butter with unsweetened applesauce, mashed banana, Greek yogurt, or pumpkin purée. Expect a softer, moister crumb.
- Butter to olive oil is a lower-saturated-fat option for savory cooking (use about 3/4 the amount).

## Sugar

Sugar is fine here. Reduce where it's painless, choose better sources where possible.

- Reduce by 25% in most cakes, muffins and quick breads without changing much; up to 50% in sauces, dressings and marinades.
- Sweeter fruit can carry the load: mashed banana, dates, applesauce.
- Maple syrup or honey can replace granulated sugar (about 3/4 the amount, reduce other liquid by ~3 tbsp per cup, lower oven temp by 25°F). They are still sugar, just less refined and more flavorful.
- Coconut sugar is comparable to regular sugar; only mention it as an alternative, not a health upgrade.
- Avoid heavy use of artificial sweeteners. If asked, stevia or monk fruit blends are options in small amounts, with a note about aftertaste and that they don't caramelize or brown like sugar.
- Flag recipes where sugar is structural (meringue, caramel, candy, yeast feeding). Don't cut it.

## Dairy

Keeping dairy is fine. Lower-fat options:

- Greek yogurt in place of sour cream, mayonnaise (part), or cream in cold sauces and dips (add at the end of cooking; heat can curdle it).
- Part-skim mozzarella or ricotta; sharper cheese in a smaller amount (Parmesan or aged cheddar gives more flavor per gram).
- Milk or unsweetened plant milk in place of cream in soups; blend in a cooked white bean or potato for body.
- Evaporated milk (low-fat) for creamy sauces.

## Sodium

- Use low-sodium broth, soy sauce (or coconut aminos), canned tomatoes and beans; rinse canned beans and vegetables.
- Cut added salt by about 25% and make up with acid (lemon, vinegar), garlic, onion, herbs, spices, umami (tomato paste, mushrooms, nutritional yeast).
- Watch cheese, olives, capers, pickles, jarred sauces, and bouillon.
- Season at the end so you can taste and stop.

## Hidden animal products

Check ingredient lists for: chicken/beef/fish stock or bouillon, fish sauce, oyster sauce, Worcestershire (anchovy), anchovy paste (in Caesar dressing and many sauces), gelatin, lard or tallow, Parmesan and other cheeses made with animal rennet, some red pesto and jarred sauces. Swap for vegetable stock, soy sauce + a splash of rice vinegar, mushroom-based oyster sauce, vegetarian Worcestershire, agar-agar, olive oil, or rennet-free cheese, and mention what was swapped.
